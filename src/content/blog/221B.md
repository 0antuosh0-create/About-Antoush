I think it was last night when I was on my phone and came across that scene from *Sherlock*. Actually, I had seen it before, and I had even saved it, but somehow, that night it felt more special.

It was the scene where Dr. Watson and Sherlock are talking, and it starts with:

> Is everyone I've ever met a psychopath?
> Yes.
> Good, now that we've settled that.
> Shut up!
> And stay shut up, because this is not funny. Not this time.

And as the conversation continues, you'll understand why.

> I didn't say it was funny.
> You.
> What have I ever done, hmm? My whole life, to deserve you?
> Everything.
> Sherlock, I've told you...
> Shut up.
> No, I mean it seriously. Everything, everything, everything you've ever done is what you did. Sherlock, one more word and you will not meet Mary.
>
> You are a doctor who went to war. You are a man who couldn't stay in the suburbs for more than a month without storming a crack den and beating up a junkie. Your best friend is a sociopath who solves crimes as an alternative to getting high. That's me, by the way, hello. Even the landlady used to run a drug cartel.
>
> It was my husband's cartel. I was just typing.
>
> John, you are addicted to a certain lifestyle. You're abnormally attracted to dangerous situations and people, so is it truly such a surprise that the woman you fall in love with conforms to that pattern?
>
> But she wasn't supposed to be like that. Why is she like that?
>
> Because you chose her.

And I don't think I need to say anything more.

But I think this happens to us more often than we want to admit. Maybe we are also "abnormally attracted to dangerous situations and people," and then, at some point, we find ourselves asking the same question:

"Is it truly such a surprise that ... conforms to that pattern?"

But just like John says:

"But she wasn't supposed to be like that. Why is she like that?"

And maybe that is the most painful part.

The fact that they weren't supposed to be like that. The version of them we had in our minds was different. The one we hoped for was different.

But they are who they are.

And we have to accept it.

And maybe that is the hardest part of it all.
